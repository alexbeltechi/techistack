# Portrait

Showing a person. The viewer wants to feel they met someone; a client or
sitter wants to see how the photographer sees people.

## Count

- 1: a single defining frame, for a feed or a series of one-image posts.
- 5–8: the usual post. One shoot, one mood.
- 10–12: only when there's real range (two looks, light changes, film and
  digital) and every frame adds something.
- More than 12 is an archive, not a post.

## Sequence

1. **Open on the face or the strongest frame.** The first image decides
   whether anyone keeps looking.
2. **Then context:** a wider frame, the place, the clothes.
3. **Then closeness:** details, hands, gestures, a laugh between poses.
4. **End on a quiet frame** or a strong black-and-white, not on a detail
   that needs explaining.

## Cut

- Near-duplicates: keep one per pose. Two only if the expression really
  differs.
- Frames that only make sense in sequence (blinks, mid-movement) unless the
  movement is the point.
- Test shots, lighting checks, behind-the-scenes, unless the post is about the
  process.
- Another person's shoot filed in the same folder.

## Mix

- Black-and-white with colour: fine when grouped or used as an opener or an
  ending, not alternating frame by frame.
- Film with digital: say so in the description; group by medium.
- Mixed orientations: lead with the orientation most of the set has.

## Words

Title with the person's name (as the user does: "Ada", "Carla Cristea").
The description, if any: when, where, what the shoot was. No adjectives about
the photos.

## Ask about

Nudity or implied nudity, minors, and anyone who might not want to be online.
